# THE TESTER PRESENT MANIFESTO
### A Code of Conduct for people who refuse to be told *"consult your dealer."*

> **Tester Present** *(service `$3E`)* — the heartbeat a diagnostic tool sends so the
> module never assumes the conversation is over, never locks the door, never goes dark.
>
> That is not just a CAN message. **That is the whole philosophy.**
> As long as someone, somewhere, keeps sending it — the line stays open.

---

## I. THE DECLARATION

They built the car. **You bought it.**

Not a *licence* to the car. Not a *subscription* to the car. The **car**. Every bolt,
every loom, every byte of firmware sitting in the module behind the dash. It is yours
the same way your house is yours and your tools are yours and the thoughts in your head
are yours.

And yet somewhere along the line we were told that the computers we *own* are somehow
still theirs. That the diagnostic port under the steering column is a privilege. That a
screen that bricks itself is a trip to the dealer and a four-figure invoice and a shrug.
That when the manufacturer walks away from a platform, the owners are supposed to walk
away too.

**We reject that. Completely. Permanently. Out loud.**

This project exists because a Ford FG Falcon's ICC is not a mystery box — it's a computer
that answers honest questions when you know how to ask them. When the official tools
vanish, when the support contracts lapse, when the only "approved" fix is *buy a new one* —
**the community picks up the tools the manufacturer put down.**

We are the tester present. We keep the session alive.

---

## II. WHAT WE BELIEVE

**1. Ownership is total.** If you paid for it, you may open it, read it, understand it,
modify it, repair it, and teach others to do the same. A serial number is not a leash.

**2. Diagnostics is a right, not a franchise.** A diagnostic port is not a confession
booth. You do not need anyone's permission to ask your own machine a question — and you
do not owe anyone a toll for the answer.

**3. Knowledge wants out.** Seed-key algorithms, memory maps, routine IDs, flash
procedures — every scrap of it that we work out, we write down, and we give away. A
secret that only a dealer network holds is a tax on everyone else. We don't pay it and we
don't collect it.

**4. Repair is dignity.** The right to fix the thing you depend on — your car, your
livelihood, your way home — is not nostalgia and it is not hobbyism. It is independence.
It is the difference between being a *customer* and being *capable*.

**5. Abandonment is not the end.** When the factory ends support, the platform does not
die — it becomes *ours*. Old iron deserves new life, and the people who keep it running
deserve the keys, not a locked glovebox.

**6. Nobody gets gatekept.** The person who just plugged in their first J2534 cable and
the person who has flashed a hundred modules are standing in the same garage. We hand over
the wrench. We do not guard the door.

---

## III. THE CODE — HOW WE TREAT EACH OTHER

A movement that's a nightmare to be a part of isn't a movement, it's a clubhouse. So here
is the deal, in plain language:

- **Share the fix, not just the flex.** Found the key? Mapped the block? Cracked the
  recore? Post *how*, not just *that you did*. "It works now 🤷" helps no one. The whole
  point is that the next person doesn't have to bleed for the same answer twice.
- **Be patient with the newcomer you used to be.** Everyone's first question is a dumb
  question to someone. None of your questions were. Answer like you'd have wanted answering.
- **Credit the shoulders you stand on.** This exists because people before you reverse-
  engineered, traced, guessed, bricked, and tried again. Name them. Link them. Thank them.
  Stealing credit in a community built on sharing is the one true heresy.
- **Disagree about the work, never degrade the worker.** Rip into a bad idea all you like.
  Bring data. Bring a trace. But the moment it turns into contempt for a *person* — their
  skill, their accent, their gear, their gender, who they love, where they're from — you've
  stopped doing engineering and started being the problem.
- **Measure twice, flash once, and say so.** You can turn a working module into a paperweight.
  When you share a procedure, share the *warnings* too. Document the failure modes. Flag the
  "wrong level permanently destroys the ICC" moments in **bold**. Honesty about risk is how
  we keep each other's cars alive.
- **Assume good faith until it's spent.** People type badly, translate roughly, and skim.
  Read the kind version first. Most "attacks" are just someone in a hurry.

---

## IV. THE LINE WE DON'T CROSS

Freedom of diagnostics is not a licence for dishonesty. This is the sharp edge of the
manifesto, and we mean it as hard as everything above:

- This is for **your** vehicle, or one you're **authorised** to work on. Full stop.
- **No odometer fraud. No VIN cloning. No immobiliser theft. No disabling safety or
  emissions systems to deceive another human being.** Rolling back a cluster to rip off a
  buyer is the exact opposite of everything here — it's using the owner's tools to betray
  the next owner.
- Right to Repair is about **truth and independence**, not fraud. The second you use this
  knowledge to lie to someone, you're not one of us — you're the reason they write the laws
  that lock the rest of us out.

Freedom with no ethics is just somebody else's crime scene. Don't be the reason the door
gets welded shut for everyone.

---

## V. WHAT GETS YOU SHOWN THE DOOR

Zero tolerance, no theatre:

- Harassment, hate, slurs, threats, or sexualised attention toward anyone, anywhere this
  community gathers.
- Doxxing, stalking, or dragging people's personal lives into a thread about CAN frames.
- Deliberately handing someone a procedure you know will brick their module. Sabotage dressed
  as help is the worst thing you can do here, and it ends your welcome instantly.
- Weaponising this knowledge for fraud or theft (see Section IV).
- Being so relentlessly, performatively toxic that good people leave rather than deal with you.
  Brilliance is not a hall pass for cruelty. We will take the kind beginner over the genius
  bully every single time.

---

## VI. ENFORCEMENT — KEEPING THE SESSION CLEAN

The maintainers are the custodians of this space, not its kings. When something goes wrong:

1. **Flag it.** Open a private report to the maintainers (see `SECURITY.md` / repo contact,
   or message a maintainer directly). Bring links, screenshots, context.
2. **It gets read by a human, in good faith, both sides.** We'd rather de-escalate than ban.
3. **Consequences scale with the act** — a quiet word, an edit, a warning, a temporary
   cool-off, or a permanent removal for anything in Sections IV–V. Sabotage, fraud, and
   hate skip straight to the end.
4. **Reporters are protected.** Retaliating against someone for raising a concern is itself
   a violation.

Decisions aim to be fair, not performative. If you think one was wrong, say so — respectfully —
and it'll be looked at again.

---

## VII. THE CREDO

> We keep our own machines.
> We ask our own questions.
> We share every answer we find.
> We lift the next person up instead of locking the door behind us.
> We tell the truth about risk, and we never use the keys to steal.
>
> When the manufacturer goes silent, **we stay present.**
> As long as one of us is still sending the signal, the module never has to go dark.
>
> **Tester present. Always.**
